About the EMI Calculator

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment: the fixed payment you make each month to repay a loan with interest. This calculator shows your monthly EMI, the total interest you will pay and the total cost of the loan from the loan amount, the yearly interest rate and the loan length.

Try different lengths to see the trade-off: a longer loan lowers the monthly payment but increases the total interest. Read the guide How EMI is calculated for the full explanation. If you are comparing prices before borrowing, the Discount Calculator and Percentage Calculator may help.

How to use it

  1. Enter the loan amount.
  2. Enter the yearly interest rate.
  3. Enter how long you will repay, in years or months.
  4. Click Calculate EMI to see the monthly payment, total interest and total cost.

How it works

  • EMI = P x r x (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)
  • P = loan amount, r = monthly interest rate (yearly rate / 12 / 100), n = number of monthly payments.
  • Total paid = EMI x n, and total interest = total paid - P. If the rate is 0%, EMI = P / n.

Worked example

A loan of 500,000 at 8.5% a year for 5 years (60 months) has an EMI of about 10,258.27. Over 60 payments you pay 615,495.94 in total, of which 115,495.94 is interest.

Common uses

  • Comparing a home, car or personal loan before applying.
  • Deciding how long a loan should run.
  • Checking whether a monthly payment fits your budget.
  • Seeing how much interest a loan adds to its price.

Limitations

  • It assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments. Floating-rate loans, part-prepayments and moratorium periods change the real figures.
  • Processing fees, insurance and taxes are not included.
  • Banks may round the EMI differently, so the figure can differ by a small amount.
  • Loan length is limited to 50 years.

Frequently asked questions

What does EMI mean?

Equated Monthly Instalment: a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and part of the loan amount.

Does a longer tenure save money?

It lowers each payment but raises the total interest, because you owe money for longer.

Is the interest calculated monthly or yearly?

The yearly rate is divided by 12 and applied each month to the balance that is still owed (the reducing balance method).

Why does my bank show a different EMI?

Banks may round the figures, add fees, or use a different compounding or day-count rule. Treat this as an estimate and confirm with your lender.
