About the Word Counter

The word counter tells you how long a piece of writing is while you type. It reports words, characters with and without spaces, sentences, paragraphs and an estimated reading time, and updates instantly with no button to press.

Length limits are everywhere: a meta description, a social post, a university essay, an application form. Counting the numbers as you write is quicker than pasting the text into a word processor each time. Need to tidy the text afterwards? Try the Case Converter or the Remove Duplicate Lines tool.

How to use it

  1. Type or paste your text into the box.
  2. The counts update as you type. There is no button to press.
  3. Use Copy results to copy the numbers, or Reset to start again.

How it works

  • Words = the number of groups of non-space characters separated by spaces, tabs or line breaks.
  • Reading time = words / 200 words per minute (an average silent reading speed, rounded up to whole minutes once it passes a minute).
  • Characters are counted as visible symbols, so an emoji counts as one character, not two.

Worked example

The text "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. It was a sunny day!" contains 14 words, 64 characters (51 without spaces) and 2 sentences.

Common uses

  • Keeping a social media post or meta description under a limit.
  • Checking an essay or assignment against a word target.
  • Estimating how long a speech or blog post will take to read aloud or silently.
  • Counting the length of product descriptions and ad copy.

Limitations

  • Words are split on spaces, so languages written without spaces (such as Chinese or Japanese) will be counted as very few words. Use the character count instead.
  • The reading time uses a single average speed. Real reading speed varies with the reader and the material.
  • Different programs count hyphenated words and numbers differently, so a result can differ by a few words from another tool.

Frequently asked questions

Does the word counter save my text?

No. Counting happens in your browser and the text is never uploaded or stored.

How is the reading time worked out?

It divides the word count by 200 words per minute, a commonly used average for silent reading.

Why does my word processor show a slightly different count?

Programs treat dashes, numbers and symbols differently. The difference is normally a handful of words.

Are spaces included in the character count?

Both numbers are shown: "Characters" includes spaces and "Characters (no spaces)" leaves them out.
