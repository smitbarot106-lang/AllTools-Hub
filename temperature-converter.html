About the Temperature Converter

The temperature converter changes a temperature between Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin. It also rejects values below absolute zero, which cannot exist.

Celsius is used in most of the world for weather and cooking, Fahrenheit is common in the United States, and Kelvin is the scientific scale. For other conversions, see the Length Converter and Weight Converter.

How to use it

  1. Type the temperature.
  2. Choose the unit you start with in "From".
  3. Choose the unit you want in "To".
  4. Click Convert.

How it works

  • Fahrenheit = Celsius x 9 / 5 + 32
  • Celsius = (Fahrenheit - 32) x 5 / 9
  • Kelvin = Celsius + 273.15

Worked examples

Water boils at 100 °C, which is 212 °F and 373.15 K. Normal human body temperature of 98.6 °F is 37 °C.

Common uses

  • Reading an oven temperature from a recipe written in another unit.
  • Understanding a weather forecast when travelling.
  • Science and homework problems that use Kelvin.

Limitations

  • Temperatures below absolute zero (-273.15 °C, -459.67 °F, 0 K) are rejected.
  • Results are shown to 10 significant digits, so tiny floating-point differences may appear in rare cases.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?

Multiply by 9, divide by 5, then add 32.

What is absolute zero?

The lowest possible temperature: 0 K, which is -273.15 °C or -459.67 °F.

At what temperature are Celsius and Fahrenheit equal?

At -40, both scales show the same number.

Why is Kelvin written without a degree sign?

Kelvin is an absolute scale, so the unit is written simply as K.
