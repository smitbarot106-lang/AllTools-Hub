How EMI is calculated

Last updated: 24 November 2026

What EMI means

EMI is the Equated Monthly Instalment: one fixed payment you make every month until a loan is repaid. Each payment covers the interest owed for that month and part of the original loan amount. Early payments are mostly interest, and later payments are mostly principal.

The EMI formula

For a loan with a fixed interest rate and equal monthly payments, the formula is:

EMI = P x r x (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)
P = loan amount; r = monthly rate = yearly rate / 12 / 100; n = number of months

Total paid = EMI x n. Total interest = total paid - P. If the interest rate is 0%, EMI is simply P / n.

A worked example

Take a loan of 1,000,000 at 9% a year for 10 years. The monthly rate is 9 / 12 / 100 = 0.0075 and the number of payments is 120.

The EMI works out to about 12,667.58. Over 120 months you pay 1,520,109.29 in total, so the interest is about 520,109.29. Use the EMI calculator to check any figures against your own loan.

How amount, rate and tenure change the EMI

  • A larger loan raises the EMI in proportion to the amount.
  • A higher interest rate raises both the EMI and the total interest.
  • A longer tenure lowers the EMI but increases the total interest, because the balance stays outstanding for longer.
  • Making a part-prepayment early in the loan usually saves more interest than one made later.

What the formula leaves out

The formula covers interest and principal only. Processing fees, insurance, taxes and changes to a floating interest rate are not included, so a lender's statement can differ. Ask for the full repayment schedule before you sign.

Frequently asked questions

Is EMI the same every month?

For a fixed-rate loan, yes. For a floating-rate loan, the EMI or the tenure can change when the rate changes.

Which is better, a shorter or longer tenure?

A shorter tenure costs less in interest but needs a higher monthly payment. Choose the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford.
